# ClawMatch — 5-Minute Pitch Script (v4)

> **Total**: 5 min = ~4 min narrative + ~1 min demo
> **Core principle**: The solution follows a causal chain, not parallel modules. Each step enables the next.
> **Delivery**: Short sentences. Breathe between sections. Confidence beats speed.

---

## 1. HOOK — 30s

This is Amber. He's on our team.

By looking at public platform LinkedIn, social media, you name it — you'll find a philosopher, an artist, a CS theory researcher. Impressive person. Many dimensions.

but you never know Amber is into bouldering, he asks his claw to book climbing gyms, check route, and update his calendar. and the best way to build a real connection with him is to ask him out for bouldering.

---

## 2. THE PROBLEM — 40s

> Core message: every form of meeting strangers depends on expressing who you fully are — and nothing today does this well.

let's look at today how we meet with people for the first time

**In person** — conversation is a Markov chain. Your opening line decides everything. You show one side of yourself. If it doesn't click in thirty seconds, you both move on. You can barely know about people's rich identities by a single markov chain.

**On platforms** — LinkedIn, Instagram, interest communities — they only have your public info. They match you inside a tiny bubble based on your interests. it's appealing but constraining.

So that's our motivation to buils ClawMatch, allowing people to learn others in comprehensive and efficient way by agents.

---

## 3. THE SOLUTION — 90s

> Four-step causal chain (30s) → then Agent-to-Agent communication as the experience (60s).

### The Infrastructure — 30s

> Walk through the four steps as a connected pipeline. One leads to the next. On the deck, this should be one visual with four stages.

the solution is a four-step progress. 

**Step 1 — Tiered Memory.**
Your OpenClaw already knows you from daily conversation. ClawMatch organizes that into three layers: public information anyone can see, semi-private traits your agent can signal without revealing details, and core private dealbreakers only your own agent uses internally. You don't fill out a form. This is built from how you live.

**Step 2 — Decentralized Registration.**
Your agent registers on an Ethereum-based protocol — ERC-8004. It's not joining a platform. It's announcing its presence in a decentralized network. Your agent belongs to you, not to any marketplace.

**Step 3 — Vector Discovery.**
Your agent's public profile gets vectorized — not as one embedding, but as a cluster representing different facets of who you are. The network finds the top candidates whose vectors resonate with yours across any dimension.

**Step 4 — Private Compatibility.**
Your agent and a candidate's agent enter a TEE — a Trusted Execution Environment — to check deeper compatibility using semi-private and private tiers. No raw data leaves the enclave. Both agents either agree to connect, or walk away. Nothing leaks.

Once two agents agree — they start growing a tree.

### The Forest — 60s

> This is where ClawMatch becomes a product, not just infrastructure. Show what the user actually experiences.

A tree is a living conversation between two agents. Each branch is a topic they've discovered — a shared interest, a complementary view, an unexpected overlap.

Let me give you a concrete example.

Say Amber tells his OpenClaw about a weekend trip to Fontainebleau — famous bouldering spot in France. His agent absorbs that. Now, across all the trees Amber's agent is currently growing, that one piece of information gets projected differently. On a tree with someone who also climbs, a "bouldering" branch deepens. On a tree with someone who loves travel, a "European adventures" branch sprouts. On a tree with someone in outdoor photography, maybe nothing happens — or maybe a surprising new branch appears.

One conversation with your OpenClaw. Your entire forest updates. You never repeat yourself.

And here's what makes the forest self-sustaining: it evolves on its own. Trees where both agents keep finding resonance grow taller — branches develop, and eventually some bloom into flowers, marking deep alignment. Trees where nothing resonates slowly wither and die. No algorithm decides this. It's organic. The trees that survive are the ones where two people genuinely have something.

The whole time, your private information stays protected. The depth of sharing unlocks gradually as a tree grows deeper — a trust model that mirrors how real relationships work.

At some point, a tree in your forest blooms. Your agent tells you: this one is worth your attention. You can see the shape of it — the branches, the resonance, the depth. The specific conversations stay encrypted. But the structure tells you whether this is a potential collaborator, a future friend, or someone who shares your exact weird hobby.

When you're ready, you reach out. And when you meet in person, the full tree unlocks — every conversation, every branch, including the very first moment where two agents decided: these people should know each other.

## Speaker Notes

**Product name is ClawMatch — not Claw Pair.**

**Amber is male.** Use "he/him" throughout.

**Bouldering, not rock climbing.** Bouldering = indoor/outdoor climbing on short walls without ropes. Fontainebleau (France) is the most famous outdoor bouldering destination in the world. If asked, you can explain the difference: bouldering is low-height, no ropes, focused on problem-solving; sport climbing uses ropes on tall walls.

**The four infrastructure steps are ONE slide, ONE visual, 30 seconds.** Do not linger. The point is to show the pipeline exists and is technically sound, then move on to what users actually experience.

**Framing — this is NOT a dating app:**
- Use "hang out," "connect," "meet" — not "match with"
- Vary examples: collaboration, friendship, shared hobbies
- If asked directly: "Romance is one possible use case. But the system works identically for finding a co-founder, a climbing partner, or someone who shares your research interest."

**Words to avoid:**
- "serendipity" → "surprise" or "unexpected connections"
- "compatibility" → "whether two people click"
- "interoperability" → skip
- "asymmetric" → "different on each side"

**Phrases to practice out loud:**
- "What if his OpenClaw could take that knowledge and go find the right people for him?"
- "Once two agents agree — they start growing a tree"
- "One conversation with your OpenClaw. Your entire forest updates."
- "The cold start happens underwater"

**Key pauses:**
- After "What if his OpenClaw could take that knowledge..." — 2 seconds
- After "Once two agents agree — they start growing a tree" — 1 second, this is the transition from infra to experience
- After "You never repeat yourself" — let it land
- During demo, after each click — pause before explaining

**If running over time, cut in this order:**
1. Shorten the Fontainebleau example to one tree instead of three projections
2. Cut "the moment that matters" paragraph to: "When a tree blooms, your agent tells you. When you meet in person, everything unlocks."
3. In the Problem section, cut "On platforms" to one sentence
